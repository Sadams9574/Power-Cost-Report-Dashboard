    Add a possible job based on a similar one

    For work you expect to chase but don't have an estimate for yet, like “we need 3 more projects in 2029.” Pick a similar job; its crew mix and shape are copied and fitted between your start and finish dates. Fill in the bottom line and click + Add job; it joins the list and a fresh line opens for the next one. Change any listed job right in its row.

    How the workbook tells the dashboard what it is Sector: a cell reading Sector with Power, Pipeline or Facilities next to it. If there is none, the file name is used (for example Pipeline_Workforce.xlsm). You can also change it in the table above. Status and Win %: a cell reading Status (Awarded, Likely, Bidding or Possible) and a cell reading Win %, each with the value next to it. Crafts tab: a tab named Crafts with the craft in column A and its group in column B (for example Welder Helper | Welding & Pipefitting). Capacity tab: a tab named Capacity with craft in column A, headcount in column B, the sector in column C and optionally weeks to hire in column D. Rows with no sector use the sector in the file name. Hiring Plan tab: a tab named Hiring Plan with craft in column A, the change in column B (+10 or −20), the starting month in column C, and optionally a sector in column D.

    Current manpower (actuals)

    People you have today in each craft, entered by sector. Company total adds the sectors together and feeds Overall and the summary at the top. Leave blank if unknown; blank crafts are left out of the shortage math. You can also type actuals in the Actual column of the shortage grid on a sector tab.
    Paste current manpower from Excel
    Copy two columns from Excel (craft, then headcount) and paste them here.

    Planned hires and releases

    Changes to headcount from a month onward, for example +10 Journeyman Electricians from Mar '27, or −20 Laborers from Nov '27. They show when “With hiring plan” is selected. A sector change shows on that sector's tab and in Overall.
